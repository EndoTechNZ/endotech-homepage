Canal-specific file sequences

← Molar collection

41 preparation courses across 12 complete molars and 40 canals. The selected finishing sizes are 25/.04 for 14 courses, 30/.04 for 14 and 35/.04 for 13. MicroPath 17/.03 starts each sequence, followed by the selected ET .04 stages.

What the evidence supports

There is no single finishing size established by a canal name. Native calibre, curvature, wall anatomy, irrigation and the instrument design all matter. MB2 research demonstrates the cost of increasing taper in thin walls; it does not establish a universal ban on one tip size. The user-identified MB2 in ACTA0115 is deliberately restricted to ET25/.04 in this teaching lesson.

  • AAE — Apical preparation has no universal standardized size; initial dimensions, anatomy and preservation of delicate curved roots affect selection. Irrigation remains necessary.
  • MB2-low-taper — In a micro-CT comparison using MB2 sizes 20, 25 and 30, design and taper affected dentine removal and transportation; findings are not interchangeable between brands.
  • MB2-25 — Size-25 MB2 preparations with different tapers/designs had different dentine preservation and perforation outcomes. A tip number alone does not establish safety.
  • MB2-taper — At size 25, larger tapers improved wall preparation but increased dentine removal; MB2 wall reduction was greater than MB1/DB in this sample.
  • palatal-width — Palatal canals in the study could have broad apical dimensions and thin curved coronal/middle walls. This supports separate assessment rather than applying an MB2 finish to every canal.

How these selections were made

Native oblique sections were sampled over the final visible 2 mm, excluding the illustrative terminal extension. The recorded widths are minimum Feret widths of the local connected lumen. They are approximate at the original 72 µm resolution; resampling adds no source detail. Narrow or strongly curved courses use a conservative smaller finish, intermediate courses use 30 and broader courses retain 35. These are explicit model-specific teaching choices within the preserved instrument set, rather than clinical prescriptions or thresholds derived from a trial. Broader oval canals may retain large untouched recesses. Increasing file size solely to colour every wall would misrepresent dentine conservation and cleaning.

ET25 uses the preserved artwork. ET20, ET30 and ET35 are the previously authorised nominal teaching derivatives. Their use does not transfer the clinical performance of another study instrument to EndoTech. These models do not establish manufacturer instructions, binding-file assessment or safe clinical preparation sizes.
