Endodontic outcomes are influenced by a limited set of biological and technical variables. TransformX™ is designed around the variables a clinician can control during preparation: apical precision, shaping quality, and procedural discipline.

  <div>

## Introduction

The evidence base for endodontic success is not built around a single file system. It is built around clinical variables that determine whether infection is controlled, anatomy is respected, and the tooth can be predictably restored.

<div class="clinical-guide-note">
  <span aria-hidden="true">i</span>
  <div>
    <strong>Evidence frame</strong>
    <p>TransformX™ positioning should be understood through the variables it supports: apical precision, shaping consistency, procedural safety, and adaptability in demanding anatomy.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Five Prognostic Domains

Across primary and secondary root canal treatment, five domains consistently influence outcome.

<div class="clinical-guide-sequence">
  <article>
    <span>01</span>
    <strong>Pre-operative status</strong>
    <p>The presence and size of pre-operative lesions affect prognosis and raise the importance of procedural control.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>02</span>
    <strong>Apical extent</strong>
    <p>Evidence supports root filling termination within 0-2 mm from the radiographic apex, with controlled termination preferred.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>03</span>
    <strong>Obturation quality</strong>
    <p>Dense, well-adapted obturation depends on the preparation form created during shaping.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="clinical-guide-feature-grid">
  <article>
    <span>Domain 4</span>
    <h3>Coronal restoration</h3>
    <p>A sealed restorative pathway protects the endodontic result from leakage and reinfection.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Domain 5</span>
    <h3>Procedural control</h3>
    <p>Transportation, ledging, perforation, and extrusion can reduce healing predictability.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Clinical link</span>
    <h3>Preparation quality</h3>
    <p>File design and sequence discipline matter because they influence shape, apical control, and treatment risk.</p>
  </article>
</div>

## Key Clinical Reference

Ricucci and Siqueira, 2011: *Apical limit of root canal instrumentation and obturation in teeth with necrotic pulps and apical periodontitis: histologic analysis.*

<div class="clinical-guide-benefits">
  <div>Highest healing rates were observed when treatment remained approximately 0.5-1.5 mm short of the apical foramen.</div>
  <div>Overfilling was associated with persistent periapical inflammation.</div>
  <div>Termination greater than 2 mm short reduced treatment success.</div>
  <div>Technical precision at the apical third affects biological healing.</div>
  <div>Instrumentation should respect the apical constriction.</div>
  <div>Preparation should remain within the biologically controlled zone.</div>
</div>
