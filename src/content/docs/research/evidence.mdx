Endodontic outcomes are influenced by a limited set of biological and technical variables. TransformX™ is designed around the variables a clinician can control during preparation: apical precision, shaping quality, and procedural discipline.

## Evidence Base

Large-scale outcome research in non-surgical root canal treatment shows that healing and long-term survival depend on biological status, technical control, and the quality of the restored seal.

| Evidence signal | Clinical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Primary RCT healing: approximately 75-83% | Absence of apical periodontitis improves prognosis, but technical control remains important. |
| Secondary RCT healing: approximately 77-80% | Retreatment outcomes remain sensitive to disinfection, apical management, and obturation quality. |
| 4-year tooth survival: approximately 95% | Tooth retention depends on endodontic control and the restorative pathway. |

*Source: Ng, UCL Eastman PhD; meta-analysis and prospective cohort.*

## Five Prognostic Domains

Across primary and secondary root canal treatment, five domains consistently influence outcome.

### 1. Pre-operative Periapical Status

The presence and size of pre-operative lesions affect prognosis. This is largely a biological factor, but it increases the importance of controlling the procedural variables that remain in the clinician's hands.

### 2. Apical Extent of Root Filling

Evidence consistently supports root filling termination within **0-2 mm from the radiographic apex**.

- **Underfilling** greater than 2 mm short can reduce disinfection and obturation quality.
- **Optimal zone** around 0.5-1.5 mm short is associated with favourable healing conditions.
- **Overfilling** beyond the apex is associated with persistent inflammation risk.

### 3. Quality of Obturation

Dense, well-adapted obturation depends on the preparation form. Shape defines the conditions available for seal.

### 4. Quality of Coronal Restoration

A sealed restorative pathway protects the endodontic result from coronal leakage and reinfection.

### 5. Intra-operative Procedural Control

Transportation, ledging, perforation, and apical extrusion can reduce healing predictability. Technical control at the apical third is therefore clinically material.

## Clinical Positioning

### Apical Control

Precision at the terminus matters because both under-preparation and over-extension can compromise healing conditions. Avatar Tip is designed to support controlled apical preparation.

### Shaping Quality

Obturation quality depends on preparation quality. A controlled taper and maintained canal path create more predictable conditions for the final seal.

### Risk Reduction

Transform Technology is designed to reduce the file behaviour that contributes to transportation and procedural error, particularly in curved anatomy.

### Structural Preservation

Controlled shaping supports conservative dentin management, which matters for the restorative prognosis and long-term tooth survival.

## Key Clinical Reference

### Ricucci and Siqueira, 2011

*Apical limit of root canal instrumentation and obturation in teeth with necrotic pulps and apical periodontitis: histologic analysis.*

Clinical highlights:

- Highest healing rates were observed when treatment remained approximately 0.5-1.5 mm short of the apical foramen.
- Overfilling was associated with persistent periapical inflammation.
- Termination greater than 2 mm short reduced treatment success.

Clinical implication: technical precision at the apical third affects biological healing. Instrumentation should respect the apical constriction and remain within the biologically controlled zone.
