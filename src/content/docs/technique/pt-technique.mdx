<p class="clinical-guide-eyebrow">Technique guide</p>

TransformX™ PT is designed for clinicians who use a ProTaper-style progressive shaping workflow and want to preserve familiar sequence logic while improving control through demanding anatomy.

<div class="clinical-guide-intro">
  <div>
    <span>Clinical aim</span>
    <strong>Shape progressively without losing control.</strong>
    <p>The PT sequence moves from coronal shaping to apical finishing while preserving working-length discipline and canal centering.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <span>System logic</span>
    <strong>Open coronally. Progress carefully. Finish only as anatomy permits.</strong>
    <p>TransformX™ PT keeps recognisable SX, S1, S2, and F-file sequence logic with added control from Transform Technology and Avatar Tip.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Introduction

Progressive taper systems are useful because they create space in a staged way. The clinical challenge is to avoid letting coronal efficiency become apical aggression, particularly when curvature or resistance increases.

<div class="clinical-guide-note">
  <span aria-hidden="true">i</span>
  <div>
    <strong>Sequence principle</strong>
    <p>PT technique works best when each file has a defined role. Do not ask shaping files to negotiate anatomy that has not first been scouted, irrigated, and confirmed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Clinical Problem

Progressive taper systems need to shape efficiently from coronal access through apical finishing. Risk increases when curvature, resistance, or incomplete glide path preparation places too much stress on the active file.

<table class="clinical-guide-table">
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Clinical risk</th>
      <th>Control response</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td>
        <strong>Coronal interference</strong>
        <span>Restricted straight-line access</span>
      </td>
      <td>Use SX conservatively to improve access without forcing apically.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>
        <strong>Mid-root resistance</strong>
        <span>Canal shape not yet developed</span>
      </td>
      <td>Progress S1 and S2 gradually with irrigation and recapitulation between files.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>
        <strong>Apical over-engagement</strong>
        <span>Final files used beyond anatomy</span>
      </td>
      <td>Select F1, F2, or F3 according to gauging, anatomy, and preparation objective.</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

## TransformX™ Solution

TransformX™ PT keeps the recognisable progressive sequence while supporting safer advancement through changing canal demands.

<div class="clinical-guide-sequence">
  <article>
    <span>01</span>
    <strong>Coronal shaping</strong>
    <p>Use SX to create early space and reduce coronal interference before moving deeper.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>02</span>
    <strong>Middle third shaping</strong>
    <p>Use S1 and S2 with frequent irrigation and recapitulation to progress toward working length.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>03</span>
    <strong>Apical finishing</strong>
    <p>Finish with F1, F2, or F3 according to apical anatomy, gauging, and clinical objective.</p>
  </article>
</div>

## Step-by-Step Sequence

<div class="clinical-guide-feature-grid">
  <article>
    <span>Step 1</span>
    <h3>Coronal shaping</h3>
    <p>Use SX at 300 RPM and 3.0 Ncm. Create initial coronal flare, improve access to the mid-root, then irrigate thoroughly.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Step 2</span>
    <h3>Middle third shaping</h3>
    <p>Use S1 and S2 at 300 RPM and 3.0 Ncm. Shape to resistance, irrigate, recapitulate, and re-irrigate before progressing.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Step 3</span>
    <h3>Apical finishing</h3>
    <p>Use F1 to working length, then select F2 or F3 only when anatomy and gauging support a larger final preparation.</p>
  </article>
</div>
