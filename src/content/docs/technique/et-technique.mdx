<p class="clinical-guide-eyebrow">Technique guide</p>

Transform S™ ET is designed for clinicians familiar with EdgeTaper, EdgeX7, Race Evo, and other .04/.06 taper workflows. The aim is to preserve a familiar sequence while adding more controlled behaviour in curved and restricted anatomy.

<div class="clinical-guide-intro">
  <div>
    <span>Clinical aim</span>
    <strong>Move from glide path to shaping without forcing.</strong>
    <p>ET technique depends on a stable path before the shaping file is asked to work at length.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <span>System logic</span>
    <strong>Glide, shape, finish.</strong>
    <p>Transform S™ ET keeps a simple .04/.06 workflow while supporting better control through Transform Technology and Avatar Tip.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Introduction

ET-style shaping is efficient when the glide path is stable and the file can progress without excessive lateral stress. Risk increases when the clinician asks the shaping file to negotiate anatomy that has not been prepared predictably.

<div class="clinical-guide-note">
  <span aria-hidden="true">i</span>
  <div>
    <strong>Technique principle</strong>
    <p>The glide path is not a formality. It is the control step that lets the ET shaping file work rather than negotiate.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Clinical Problem

ET-style systems can feel familiar and efficient, but control depends on sequence discipline.

<table class="clinical-guide-table">
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Clinical risk</th>
      <th>Control response</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td>
        <strong>Incomplete glide path</strong>
        <span>Shaping file carries negotiation load</span>
      </td>
      <td>Confirm a reproducible path before ET 20/.04 is introduced.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>
        <strong>Curved anatomy</strong>
        <span>Lateral stress and transportation risk</span>
      </td>
      <td>Use light pressure, irrigation, and recapitulation between files.</td>

    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

## Transform S™ Solution

Transform S™ ET places glide path confirmation first, then uses a simple shaping and finishing sequence supported by Transform Technology and Avatar Tip.

<div class="clinical-guide-sequence">
  <article>
    <span>01</span>
    <strong>Glide path</strong>
    <p>Use Micro-Path™ 13/.03 to establish a reproducible path to working length before shaping.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>02</span>
    <strong>Shaping</strong>
    <p>Use ET 20/.04 with light apical pressure, irrigation, and recapitulation.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>03</span>
    <strong>Finishing</strong>
    <p>Use ET 25/.04, then select larger sizes only when anatomy and gauging support a larger final preparation.</p>
  </article>
</div>

## Step-by-Step Sequence

<div class="clinical-guide-feature-grid">
  <article>
    <span>Step 1</span>
    <h3>Glide path</h3>
    <p>Use Micro-Path™ 13/.03 at 300 RPM and 2.0-2.5 Ncm. Establish a reproducible path to working length, then irrigate thoroughly.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Step 2</span>
    <h3>Shaping</h3>
    <p>Use ET 20/.04 at 350 RPM and 3.0 Ncm. Shape to working length with light pressure, then irrigate and recapitulate.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Step 3</span>
    <h3>Finishing</h3>
    <p>Use ET 25/.04 at 350 RPM and 3.0 Ncm. Complete apical sizing without forcing beyond the prepared path.</p>
  </article>
</div>
