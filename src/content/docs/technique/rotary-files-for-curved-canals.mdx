Rotary files for curved canals must do more than cut efficiently. They need to preserve the original canal path while limiting transportation, wall stress, cyclic fatigue, and abrupt file behaviour in the apical third.

## Clinical Problem

Curved canals increase the consequences of every mechanical decision.

| Risk in curved anatomy | Clinical consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Straightening tendency | Loss of the original canal path and increased transportation risk. |
| Lateral wall stress | Higher demand on dentin and file fatigue resistance. |
| Incomplete glide path | Shaping files carry too much negotiation load. |
| Aggressive apical engagement | Greater risk of ledging, zipping, or loss of working-length control. |

## TransformX™ Solution

TransformX™ ET combines familiar ET-style sequence logic with two control-focused design elements: [Transform Technology](../../technology/transform-technology/) and [Avatar Tip](../../technology/avatar-tip/).

The practical sequence is straightforward:

1. Establish a reproducible glide path with [Acrobat Glide Path Files](../../products/acrobat-glide-path/).
2. Shape with TransformX™ ET while maintaining light apical pressure and frequent irrigation.
3. Let Avatar Tip support centred apical progression as curvature becomes more demanding.

## Key Features

### Glide Path Control

A controlled glide path reduces the negotiation burden placed on shaping files. This is especially important in canals where curvature begins early or intensifies near the apex.

### Metallurgical Adaptability

Transform Technology is designed to vary file response along the working length. Clinically, this supports greater flexibility toward the tip while retaining shaping stability coronally.

### Tip Guidance

Avatar Tip is designed to support smoother, more centred apical progression in the most demanding part of the canal.

## Clinical Benefits

When glide path, metallurgy, and tip design are aligned, rotary files can support:

- Better apical control.
- Improved canal centering.
- More predictable progression to working length.
- Reduced tendency toward abrupt outer-wall engagement.
- A cleaner transition from negotiation to shaping.
